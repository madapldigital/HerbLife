The situation

Herb Life has built a real brand across four Durban stores: the vibe, the "Durbz" voice, the loyal Instagram following. But like every cannabis retailer, that brand can't run the paid ads that would normally carry it further. Word of mouth and Google reviews are doing the heavy lifting instead, whether or not anyone's actively managing them.

That's not a small gap to leave unmanaged. 81% of consumers check Google reviews before visiting a business, and over half look for an average rating of at least 4 out of 5 stars before they'll even consider walking in. A single extra star on that rating has been shown to lift revenue by 5 to 9%, and each additional Google review drives roughly 80 more website visits, 63 direction requests, and 16 calls on its own, according to 2025 industry research.

Meanwhile, the in-store basics (WiFi at the counter, explaining the membership model to new visitors, tracking who's a regular) still run on paper and memory, store by store, with no easy way to prompt a review while the visit is still fresh.

What ClubTap does for Herb Life

A small set of NFC tags around the Hillcrest store, each opening a specific hosted page the moment a phone taps it. No app for customers to download, just a tap. This matters more than it sounds: industry data shows NFC completes 60 to 80% of the time at the point of service, against 35 to 50% for QR codes, because a tap is one action where a QR code needs someone to open their camera, scan, wait, and confirm. Fewer steps between "happy customer" and "review submitted" means more reviews actually land.

Google Reviews
